# Residual obligations — disposition record

Status: **structurally present, semantically degenerate.** This record states what
exists, why the current issuance reports `unrepresented`, and what a future campaign
would have to build. Nothing here is an implementation order, and no placeholder
obligation should be added to make the seam look complete.

## What exists now

The pure kernel's effect crossing is a conjunction over four families — standing,
custody, **obligation**, capacity — generated by one macro
(`crates/ag-kernel/src/family.rs`). The obligation family is therefore complete in
shape: it has a `ObligationBook`, `ObligationRef`, `ObligationClaim`,
`ObligationWitness`, `ObligationRefusal`, and `ObligationFailure`, all with the same
four failure variants as its siblings, and the crossing genuinely evaluates it (all
four families are evaluated and refusals accumulate; no short-circuit).

What it does **not** have is content.

- The only production caller that builds the books, `worker_effect_evaluation`
  (`crates/ag-app/src/agd.rs`), populates all four with the *same* subject digest and a
  hardcoded book-local id (`"worker-reviewed-mapping-v1"` for obligation).
- `ensure_claim_subject` (`crates/ag-app/src/agd.rs`) then *requires* all four claims to
  carry that same subject digest.

Together those mean the four-family crossing currently decides **one thing four times**.
An obligation entry records that a subject exists, not that anything is owed.

Nothing reads an obligation result for its own sake: the obligation witness is carried
into `Authority` reconstruction and compared for equality, never interpreted.

The nearest thing to a live post-decision duty is the proposal lifecycle state
`ReconciliationRequired` (`crates/ag-effect/src/lib.rs`), which requires an operator to
supply reconciliation evidence. That is a real obligation on a human — but it is
modelled as a lifecycle state, not as an obligation record, and it never reaches the
obligation book or an issuance.

## Answers to the audit questions

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| Can a real decision produce a meaningful non-empty residual? | **No.** The only production book-builder emits subject echoes. |
| Does any residual affect authorization, issuance, burn, or downstream behaviour? | **No.** It participates in the crossing's conjunction and in witness equality, nothing more. |
| Are the four books semantically distinct? | **In type, yes** (non-interchangeable refs, compile-fail-tested). **In content, no** — one subject, four books. |
| Is "no residual" distinguishable from "residual unavailable"? | **Only at the issuance boundary**, where `ResidualStatusV1` distinguishes `none_recorded` from `unrepresented`. Inside the kernel there is no such distinction. |
| Which parts are inherited migration structure? | None. This is formal-correspondence structure (the four-family crossing crosswalked to the calculus), not AG-classic residue. |
| Which classic doctrine was dropped? | AG classic had three *live* residual mechanisms — non-discharge claims bound into receipt identity, policy obligations with enforcement records, and tolerability horizons. None survived the hard cut, deliberately and without replacement. |

## Why the historical V1 issuance reported `unrepresented`

Because it was true. The now-retired `ag.docket-issuance:v1` carried a residual
**status**, and its former producer emitted `unrepresented` — *"this producer cannot
express residual obligations"* —
rather than `none_recorded`, which would assert that the decision produced none. The
consumer stores that status verbatim and the downstream testimony consumer renders it as
a coverage limitation ("their absence is a producer limitation, not evidence"). The
seam is therefore **honest but not lossless** on residuals, and says so at every hop.

## Ceremony versus useful substrate

**Useful historical substrate, keep as evidence:** the type-level separation of the four families
(non-interchangeable refs, per-family refusal types, lossless accumulation); the
`ResidualStatusV1` three-way status on the wire; the `UpstreamResidualV1` shape already
carried end to end (source system, obligation id, subject, kind, statement), which is
exactly what a downstream office needs and is already exercised by a passing specimen.

**Ceremony, do not mistake for capability:** the obligation book *as currently
populated*. It is not wrong — the shape is right — but it currently asserts nothing.

**Do not delete either.** Deleting the obligation family would break the kernel's
crossing shape and its calculus crosswalk; deleting the wire status would remove the
one place the limitation is stated honestly.

## Minimum mechanism to re-earn

A meaningful residual needs, at minimum:

1. **A source of obligation content.** Something must *decide* that a duty is owed —
   a policy rule, a target-catalog attribute, or a reviewed mapping — and produce an
   obligation with its own subject and statement, distinct from the crossing's subject
   echo. This is the missing piece, and it is a policy-doctrine choice, not a
   refactor.
2. **A distinct book-local identity per obligation**, so the book records obligations
   rather than one hardcoded row.
3. **A discharge story, or an explicit refusal to have one.** Either obligations can be
   discharged (by what evidence, under whose authority?) or the system states that they
   never are and only accumulate. Both are defensible; silence is not.
4. **A consumer that behaves differently.** If no downstream office acts differently on
   a present versus absent residual, the mechanism is ceremony regardless of its
   content.

## What must survive into the consuming offices

Already true and must not regress: the residual **status** distinction; the
upstream-vocabulary residual shape carried without coercion into the consumer's own
obligation type; `discharged: false` structurally; and downstream rendering as a
coverage limitation rather than as a claim.

## Acceptance tests for a future re-earning campaign

1. A real decision produces a non-empty obligation whose subject differs from the
   crossing subject, with a distinct book-local identity.
2. Two decisions with different inputs produce different obligation content (proving it
   is not a constant).
3. The issuance carries `present` with those items, and tampering with any of them
   breaks authentication (already true of the wire; must remain).
4. The consumer stores them undischarged and its dossier renders them as upstream
   obligations, not as its own.
5. The downstream office renders each as an outstanding coverage limitation, and a
   source-marked-discharged residual still refuses (already true; must remain).
6. An obligation refusal at the crossing blocks admission, with a test proving a
   proposal that would otherwise admit does not.

## Verdict

**RE-EARN through a separate design campaign.** Not live, not deletable, not migration
residue. Keep the shape and the honest status; do not add placeholder obligations; do
not let the seam claim losslessness until criterion 1 above has an answer that is a
policy decision rather than a refactor.
