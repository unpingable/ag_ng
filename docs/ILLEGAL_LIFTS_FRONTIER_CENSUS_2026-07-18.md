## Three independent lanes

1. **Preparation and ratification.** Exact candidate/basis/input binding,
   protected-projection preservation, candidate-specific ratification, and
   promotion through separately held standing.
2. **Inquiry attempt history.** Separately authorized attempts, append-only
   acquisition outcomes, testimony decisions, obligation discharge, and the
   refusal of final-success-only history laundering. Shape laundering belongs
   at this acquisition boundary.
3. **Historical versus present authority.** Historical validity,
   retrospective assessability, and current governability remain distinct;
   replay and recovery preserve recorded claims without resurrecting standing.

Proof/runtime correspondence cuts across the lanes as a membrane obligation.
It remains resident in AG-ng's explicit non-authorizing calculus adapter rather
than becoming a fourth semantic hierarchy.

## Preparation-kernel scope correction

Same basis and complete inputs do **not** imply artifact-byte uniqueness unless
deterministic construction is an explicit premise. Timestamps, random choices,
solver choices, or external acquisition may legitimately produce distinct
artifacts from the same declared inputs.

The safe initial contract is:

- every candidate carries one exact preparation-basis/input binding;
- ratification names that exact candidate and binding;
- content equality does not imply basis equality;
- eligibility is candidate-specific;
- a later deterministic-preparation instance may prove a stronger uniqueness
  result under an explicit determinism premise.

The forcing hostile fixture is two byte-identical artifacts prepared against
different bases, with ratification and promotion standing for only one. Byte
equality cannot transport the other candidate's eligibility.

The first preparation packet should model a grant, a prepared candidate with
artifact/basis/input/receipt/residue evidence, candidate-and-basis ratification,
a separately scoped promotion grant, and a current-basis check. Its initial
laws are:

- preparation does not mint promotion standing;
- preparation preserves the protected authoritative projection;
- ratification binds the exact candidate and basis;
- byte equality cannot transport ratification;
- basis mismatch refuses;
- candidate mutation invalidates ratification;
- successful compare-and-swap establishes exclusivity, not promotion
  authority.

No generic commutation or artifact-uniqueness theorem is in scope.

## Inquiry-attempt packet

The second packet belongs inside the append-only inquiry lifecycle. It keeps
these events distinct:

```text
InquiryOpened
AttemptAuthorized
AttemptRecorded
ArtifactAcquired
ArtifactTestifies
ObligationDischarged
```

No event implies its successor without an explicit bridge. Acquisition failure
is not semantic refusal, acquired structure is not testimony, and one final
success cannot erase prior failures, changed basis, exhausted budget, or
unauthorized retries.

The forcing fixture is two acquisition failures followed by successful
testimony. A final-success-only projection is insufficient to establish that
the inquiry qualified within its authorized envelope.

`Bridge owner` is load-bearing. A mathematical function does not establish
operational legitimacy; the operational bridge additionally requires current
standing, provenance, scope, policy ownership, and exact evidence.

No atlas entry may be extracted into a formal campaign without a concrete
forcing case. Conceptual appeal, analogy, or an available theorem name is not a
forcing case.

## Initial ranked entries

### 1. Post-witness substitution / exact-basis promotion

- **Source judgment:** candidate prepared under one exact basis and input set.
- **Target judgment:** another equal-looking candidate may be promoted.
- **Forbidden lift:** preparation or byte equality implies promotion
  eligibility.
- **Bridge owner:** promotion policy in the current authority domain.
- **Bridge evidence:** exact preparation receipt, candidate/basis ratification,
  current promotion grant, epoch, and current-basis observation.
- **Basis/epoch:** exact preparation basis plus current promotion epoch.
- **Hostile fixture:** same bytes, different basis, one ratification.
- **Existing residence:** AG-ng worker custody and managed-pointer preparation,
  ratification, prestate, CAS, and mismatch refusal.
- **Disposition:** extract now as a compact sibling kernel.
- **Forcing case:** AG-ng's closed promotion loop.

### 2. Attempt laundering

- **Source judgment:** one attempt eventually produced qualified testimony.
- **Target judgment:** the inquiry qualified cleanly and within its envelope.
- **Forbidden lift:** final success erases attempt history.
- **Bridge owner:** inquiry policy and its attempt-budget authority.
- **Bridge evidence:** opened inquiry, per-attempt authorization, append-only
  outcomes, testimony decision, and discharge receipt.
- **Basis/epoch:** inquiry basis, attempt nonce/order, policy epoch, and budget.
- **Hostile fixture:** failure, failure, success.
- **Existing residence:** dynamic inquiry skunkworks campaign.
- **Disposition:** extract now inside the inquiry lifecycle.
- **Forcing case:** retrying acquisition and qualification workflows.

### 3. Shape laundering

- **Source judgment:** an `InquiryGrantV0`-shaped document parsed successfully.
- **Target judgment:** the inquiry was authorized.
- **Forbidden lift:** structural validity implies standing.
- **Bridge owner:** the mandate issuer's authority domain.
- **Bridge evidence:** authenticated issuer, mandate, domain, epoch, nonce,
  origin, obligation, and attempt authorization.
- **Basis/epoch:** grant schema and current issuer/policy epoch.
- **Hostile fixture:** locally fabricated valid JSON.
- **Existing residence:** NQ-shaped acquisition envelope and AG-ng signed
  principal/epoch boundaries.
- **Disposition:** resident requirement; extract with the inquiry packet, not as
  a standalone grand theory.
- **Forcing case:** NQ-to-inquiry grant ingestion.

### 4. Proof-carrying irrelevance

- **Source judgment:** a named Lean theorem holds for its formal model.
- **Target judgment:** a Rust transition is authorized.
- **Forbidden lift:** theoremhood implies runtime authority.
- **Bridge owner:** the runtime correspondence and effect-policy owners.
- **Bridge evidence:** schema and interpretation correspondence, version pin,
  executable conformance evidence, exact theorem/model identity, and disclosed
  gaps.
- **Basis/epoch:** Lean revision, adapter schema, evaluator identity, runtime
  domain, and epoch.
- **Hostile fixture:** theorem-shaped or correctly signed bytes presented
  without runtime custody.
- **Existing residence:** AG-ng's formal-calculus crosswalk, obligation ledger,
  and non-authorizing adapter.
- **Disposition:** resident; do not re-extract.
- **Forcing case:** every calculus-informed AG-ng decision boundary.

### 5. Authority resurrection

- **Source judgment:** a historical decision can be replayed or assessed.
- **Target judgment:** it has current standing.
- **Forbidden lift:** historical validity or reproducibility implies present
  governability.
- **Bridge owner:** current activation/recovery authority.
- **Bridge evidence:** current domain/epoch, recovery cut, standing, policy,
  migration custody, and replay classification.
- **Basis/epoch:** original history cut and current recovery epoch, both exact.
- **Hostile fixture:** valid classic receipt or copied pre-consumption store
  presented after restart/restore.
- **Existing residence:** AG-ng frozen archive, restart recovery, replay,
  migration, and restore fences.
- **Disposition:** next larger extraction after preparation and inquiry stabilize.
- **Forcing case:** cold archive, daemon restart, backup restore, and legacy
  migration.
